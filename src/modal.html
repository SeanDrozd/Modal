Room wave interference

Single-frequency sound pressure field, evaluated at listener ear height, via an exact rectangular-room modal solution below 300Hz (bass-focused; wall, floor, and ceiling reflections come from each surface's material, not individual bounces). Sources roll off outside their frequency range at a configurable slope (24 dB/octave by default).

Saved configurations
▾Room
Width/depth up to 30ft, ceiling height up to 15ft — beyond that, this tool's 1-2 sub optimizer and handful of ranked listening positions stop being a meaningful fit for the room, well before the modal solution's own math or GPU limits would actually break.
▾Listening Positions
A "row" is a group of seats (a couch, a row of recliners) — rank is just this list's order (drag/reorder with the arrows below): the top row matters most, and it fades from there. Within a row, the center seat matters most, tapering toward the ends. Position is set by dragging each row's marker on the map.
▾Sources

Assumes you have a way to actually apply per-source delay in real life (an AVR's speaker-distance trim, or DSP) — see the README for the practical caveats.

▾Frequency response
Spatially averaged over a small sphere around the listening position (20 points, 2ft radius), approximating a moving-mic-style measurement rather than one exact point, which can look artificially nulled or peaked. Assumes anechoically flat sources; shows only what each source's own configured range/rolloff and the room (reflections, absorption) do to the signal.

▾Walls
Ordinary drywall-on-studs construction absorbs more bass right around its own resonance (damped further by cavity insulation) than it does elsewhere — rigid materials like masonry stay close to flat across the whole range. These are typical/illustrative values, not measured for your exact walls.
