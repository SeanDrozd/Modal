Room wave interference

Single-frequency sound pressure field, evaluated at listener ear height. Includes wall, floor, and ceiling reflections (up to 3rd order), with optional wall openings and absorption panels. Sources roll off outside their frequency range at a configurable slope (24 dB/octave by default).

Absorption panels
Panels stick to a wall and run parallel to it. Thicker panels absorb lower frequencies more effectively (quarter-wavelength rule) — the inward band on the map shows how effective each panel is at the current frequency.
Openings
An opening sits on a wall (any wall, any position) and reduces that wall's reflection near it — e.g. a doorway or open floorplan. Add any number, move them, or remove them entirely.
Walls
Ordinary drywall-on-studs construction absorbs more bass right around its own resonance (damped further by cavity insulation) than it does elsewhere — rigid materials like masonry stay close to flat across the whole range. These are typical/illustrative values, not measured for your exact walls.

Reflections
2nd order adds corner reflections (bounces off two boundaries before reaching the listener) — more accurate near actual room corners, at the cost of redraw speed. 3rd order adds a further bounce (~5x more image sources per source than 2nd order) — noticeably slower, and with many sources active at once (subs, panels) GPU rendering may start truncating/warning.
to
Narrower ranges show subtle differences more clearly; wider ranges keep deep nulls distinguishable from each other instead of all clipping to the same color.
