Modal

Subwoofer placement and room mode modeling
    Modal is designed for tablets and larger screens. It runs here, but the controls will be cramped.

    Single-frequency sound pressure field, evaluated at listener ear height, via an exact rectangular-room modal solution below 300Hz (bass-focused; wall, floor, and ceiling reflections come from each surface's material, not individual bounces). Sources roll off outside their frequency range at a configurable slope (24 dB/octave by default).

    Saved configurations
    ▾Room
    Width/depth up to 30ft, ceiling height up to 15ft. Beyond that, this tool's sub optimizer and handful of ranked listening positions stop being a meaningful fit for the room.
    ▾Walls
    Ordinary drywall-on-studs construction absorbs more bass right around its own resonance (damped further by cavity insulation) than it does elsewhere. Rigid materials like masonry stay close to flat across the whole range. These are typical/illustrative values, not measured for your exact walls.

    Suspended acoustic tile absorbs broadband (more at higher frequencies) rather than resonating like drywall, so it does relatively little within this tool's bass-focused range.
    ▾Speakers & subs
    When on, every source's delay (below) is automatically kept time-aligned, so its direct sound arrives at the listening position together with the farthest source, recalculated whenever a source or listening position moves. A manually-set delay sticks until the next such change, useful for briefly detuning and watching the frequency response chart, but it won't persist past that. Turn it off to set every delay by hand and have it stay put. Assumes you have a way to actually apply per-source delay in real life, such as an AVR's speaker-distance trim or DSP; see the README for the practical caveats.
    The dashed triangle on the map connects whichever two sources are marked "Front L/R" below to the listening position. Check that box on exactly two sources (typically your main stereo pair) to see it, regardless of how many other sources (subs, etc.) are also present.
    ▾Seats
    A "row" is a group of seats (a couch, a row of recliners), rank is just this list's order (drag/reorder with the arrows below): the top row matters most, and it fades from there. Within a row, the center seat matters most, tapering toward the ends. Position is set by dragging each row's marker on the map.
    ▾Frequency response
    Spatially averaged over a small sphere around the listening position (20 points, 2ft radius), approximating a moving-mic-style measurement rather than one exact point, which can look artificially nulled or peaked. Assumes anechoically flat sources; shows only what each source's own configured range/rolloff and the room (reflections, absorption) do to the signal.
    to
    Narrower ranges show subtle differences more clearly; wider ranges keep deep nulls distinguishable from each other instead of all clipping to the same color. Also sets this chart's own dB axis. If the heatmap looks solid one color, or the chart looks like a flat line, click Autofit.
    ▾Furniture & zones
    Add furniture, doorways and walkways where a sub can't go. Furniture is not included in the acoustic calculation. It only excludes sub positions.
    Click a button, then click-drag on the map to draw a zone. Drag a zone's edge/corner to resize, drag its middle to move.
    ▾Optimizer
    Balances average level and flatness across the band. Switch to "Null avoidance" if you have room correction/EQ available: it ignores level and peaks (which EQ can fix) and scores purely by the deepest null, which EQ can't fill in.
    to
    Refines your existing subs' own positions in place, starting from where they already are -- a sub that's already well placed can stay put; only add-new-subs uses "Find best sub position" above.
